# Style study: post-classical ambient jazz

What GoGo Penguin, Ólafur Arnalds, Hania Rani, Svaneborg Kardyb, Jakob Bro, Eivind Aarset, Dominic Miller and Lambert
have in common, in terms a producer can use: instruments, harmony, time, form, sound — and how to make it in this
studio. Written in November 2026 from interviews, reviews and one published score analysis (linked at the end),
together with what is generally known about these records.

**How far to trust it.** Nothing here was measured from audio: no track was analysed in this session. Statements
with a link are from that source. The rest — most of the harmony, tempo and form sections — is a description from
knowledge of the music, not a measurement, and is marked *(unmeasured)* where it gives numbers. Upload three to five
tracks and the [song-study workflow](../song-studies.md) will check tempo, key, chords and form against this page.

## In one paragraph

A **soft, close-recorded acoustic instrument** (a felted or muted piano, a clean guitar, a Wurlitzer) plays a **short
figure that repeats and changes one thing at a time**, over **slow, mostly diatonic harmony coloured with added
notes** (9ths, sus, major 7ths, 11ths) rather than with jazz cadences; **time is either a quiet steady pulse or no
pulse at all**, never swing and never a backbeat; the piece **grows by adding and removing layers**, not by changing
section; **electronics are made from the acoustic sound itself** (loops, pads, granular and reversed layers) and sit
behind it; and **room, decay and the instrument's own noises** are left in as part of the music. Jazz supplies the
touch, the interplay and the freedom to leave things out; classical supplies the restraint and the writing;
minimalism supplies the process.

## Where it sits: three houses

The eight names come from three label worlds, and the style is the place where they overlap.

| House | Who | What it brings |
|---|---|---|
| **ECM** (Munich; much of it recorded in Oslo) | Jakob Bro, Eivind Aarset, Dominic Miller | Space. A producer-led sound of "purity, the judicious use of silence" and live takes rather than overdubs; reverb on most records; a balance where no instrument leads ([DownBeat](https://downbeat.com/news/detail/ecm-manfred-eicher-search-for-sublime), [ECM Reviews](https://ecmreviews.com/tag/steve-lake/)) |
| **Gondwana** (Manchester) | GoGo Penguin (early records), Hania Rani, Svaneborg Kardyb | Pulse. A label for "meditative-spiritual" jazz, "neo-classical, ambient and electronic music" ([HHV Mag](https://hhv-mag.com/feature/gondwana-records-soul-and-character/?lang=en)): minimalism and electronica played by hand |
| **Erased Tapes / Mercury KX** | Ólafur Arnalds, Lambert | Intimacy. The piano as a close, soft, imperfect object; strings and electronics around it |

## The common denominators

### Instruments and roles

- **One soft lead voice** carries nearly everything: felted or softly played piano, clean electric or nylon guitar,
  Wurlitzer. It is recorded close, so it is quiet *and* detailed.
- **Bass** is an acoustic double bass playing long notes or a repeating figure, sometimes bowed — or there is no
  bass and the left hand does the job.
- **Drums**, when present, are colour first and time second: brushes, mallets, cymbal washes, rim clicks; or a
  tight, dry, quiet pattern that imitates programmed drums. No loud backbeat.
- **Strings** are a quartet, not an orchestra: held notes, slow swells, entrances that are barely noticed.
- **Electronics are derived from the acoustic sound**: a pad that doubles the chord an octave below, a loop of the
  phrase just played, a granular or reversed copy. They fill the gaps and never take the foreground.
- A wordless or half-sung voice, a breathy trumpet, a vibraphone may appear as a second colour. Rarely more than
  four things sound at once.

### Harmony

- **Minor-centred and diatonic**: natural minor and dorian most often, lydian and major for the lighter pieces. The
  leading tone and the dominant seventh are mostly absent — which is much of why it sounds melancholy without drama.
- **Slow harmonic rhythm**: a chord lasts one to four bars; a whole piece is often two to four chords.
- **Colour by adding, not by altering**: add9, sus2, sus4, 6/9, major 7, minor 7, minor 9, minor 11, open fifths,
  stacks of fourths. Altered dominants (♭9, ♯9, ♭13) and tritone substitutes — the bebop vocabulary — are rare.
- **Movement by step and by third**: i–VI–III–VII and its rotations, i–v, IV–I, ♭VII–I; a bass that walks down by
  step under chords that hardly change (inversions); a held note or drone under moving harmony.
- **Borrowed chords are an event**, saved for a later section. In Hania Rani's *Glass* the first sections are
  diatonic and the borrowed chords first appear in the third ([IJAD paper](https://ir.uitm.edu.my/97490/1/97490.pdf)).
- **Voice leading is minimal**: common tones stay, the others move a step. The top note of the chords is the tune.

### Melody

- **Cells, not themes**: two to five notes, stepwise or pentatonic, repeated with one change at a time. *Glass* has
  three melodic patterns that return through a section, and 42 small variants of the melody in all (same paper).
- The melody is often simply **the top of the repeating figure**; a separate "lead line" may never arrive.
- **Long notes and rests** between phrases; a phrase is allowed to end without an answer.

### Time

Two poles, and many pieces move from one to the other:

- **Pulse**: a figure in straight eighths or sixteenths at a moderate tempo — *Glass* is in 4/4 at 108 BPM in
  C♯ minor (same paper); roughly 95–130 BPM is typical *(unmeasured)*. The interest is in grouping (3+3+2, three
  against two, an odd bar now and then), not in a groove. Drums often play at half the speed of the piano.
- **Breath**: no steady pulse — rubato, held chords, phrases as long as a breath. Roughly 55–80 BPM if counted at
  all *(unmeasured)*.
- Never swing. Dynamics do the work a groove does elsewhere.

### Form — the common structures

Not verse and chorus. Four shapes cover most of it:

1. **Additive arc** — one idea, grown and undone:
   figure alone → + left hand or bass → + pulse (drums, a second layer) → peak by register and density, same
   harmony → layers leave one by one → the figure alone, then its decay. 3 to 6 minutes.
2. **Two panels** — stillness, then motion: a slow, free opening; the pulse arrives; the opening returns briefly
   at the end.
3. **Tone poem** — a texture with a tune stated once or twice and no development: the piece is the sound of the
   room. The ECM guitar records live here.
4. **Process piece** — a pattern that phases, lengthens or shifts against itself, as in the minimalists; the form
   is the process.

Shared habits inside them: phrases of 2, 4 or 8 bars, with changes every two bars (*Glass*: a left-hand ostinato
that changes every 2 bars, sections of 8 to 20 bars, a later section that repeats the first three — same paper);
**the climax is louder, higher and denser, not a new key**; endings fade or simply stop and ring; silence before
the last phrase.

### Sound

- **Close and quiet**: felt between hammers and strings, soft playing, microphones near enough to hear the
  mechanism. The noises stay in.
- **Reverb as a place**: long, dark, behind the instrument rather than on it — the ECM tradition.
- **Soft top end, slow movement**: tape-like saturation and a little pitch drift, high frequencies rolled off,
  nothing bright or hard.
- **Wide and layered**: two pianos left and right (Stratus), doubled takes, a pad under everything.
- **Dynamic and quiet overall**: little compression; the loud part of a piece is still not loud *(unmeasured)*.

### What is not there

Swing; walking bass; head–solos–head; fast runs shown off; verse–chorus hooks; loud snare on two and four;
bright synth leads; side-chain pumping; harmonic-minor cadences; lyrics that tell a story.

## Three dialects

| Dialect | Who | Tempo / time | Signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hand-played electronica** | GoGo Penguin, Svaneborg Kardyb, Rani's *Glass* | steady pulse | piano or Wurlitzer ostinato, dry tight drums, bass figure, builds |
| **Felt and strings** | Ólafur Arnalds, Lambert, most of Hania Rani | slow, rubato | felted piano, string quartet swells, tape and pads |
| **ECM guitar** | Jakob Bro, Eivind Aarset, Dominic Miller | free or very slow | clean or nylon guitar, loops and washes, cymbals as weather |

## Making it in GGS

**The template.** *New from template → Post-classical ambient jazz* (`create_project template="postclassical"`)
opens **"Winter Light"**: 72 bars in E minor at 108 BPM, a little under three minutes, built the way this page
describes. A felt-piano figure in 3+3+2 opens alone and thinned out; the left hand joins, then an upright bass and a
pad, then strings and a three-note vibraphone cell on four other chords — the last of them A major, borrowed, whose
C♯ is the only note from outside the key; at the peak a second piano answers from left and right in quiet
sixteenths, a shaker and a rim keep time as colour and a cello has the one line that could be called a tune; then
the layers leave in turn and the first chord is rolled once and left to ring. The chords are the chord grid's
(Two Hands voicing for the piano, Open for strings and pad, led by "Follow"). Rendered by the studio it measures
−19.9 LUFS with peaks at −3 dB, the limiter never working, and 9 dB between the opening and the peak. It has been
checked by its notes and by the meter, not by ear: use it as a place to start and trust your own.

The recipe behind it — a starting point for Claude or for you; change one thing to make it yours.

| | Choice |
|---|---|
| **Tempo and key** | 60–76 BPM for the still pieces, 100–118 for the pulse pieces; natural minor or dorian (C♯, D, F, A minor), lydian for light |
| **Lead** | Grand Piano preset **Felt** (or **Upright**), played soft (velocity 0.35–0.6); or Guitar **Nylon** / **Clean Electric**; or Electric Piano **Wurli** |
| **Bass** | Bass Guitar preset **Upright**: one long note per chord, or a two-bar figure |
| **Strings and pads** | String Ensemble **Slow Strings** or **Solo Cello** entering late and quietly; GGS Synth **Velvet Pad** or Additive **Glass Pad** an octave under the chords; Modal **Vibraphone** / **Bowed Bars** as a second colour |
| **Drums** | none; or rim, shaker and cymbal only, soft, half-time under the figure |
| **Chords** | from the chord grid (`chord_grid`, Harmony → Grid): add9, sus2, M7, m9, 6/9 on the degrees; the Ext page for m11 and M7♯11; **no tensions on dominants**; one borrowed chord (iv, ♭VI or ♭VII) kept for the last third |
| **Voicing** | `add_grid_chords` voicing **open** or **piano**, leading **follow**, strum 25–45 ms, humanize 15–25 |
| **Figure** | `add_arpeggio` on the same chords (modes `converge`, `pinky`, `updown`; rate 1/8 or 1/16; a little swing 0) for the pulse pieces; change one note of it every two bars |
| **Form** | the additive arc: 4–8 bars figure alone, then a layer every 4–8 bars, peak two thirds in, layers out, last chord left to ring |
| **Space** | `algoverb` as a shared send (decay 3–5 s, pre-delay 20–40 ms, damped); `shimmer` only on a pad, low; `lofi` for tape drift on the piano; `delay` in dotted eighths, quiet, on the guitar |
| **Level** | no limiter pushing: leave the dynamics, target `apple-music` or quieter in `render_mix` |

Per-note expression (pitch and pressure curves) on the pad and the cello is what keeps held notes alive; see
[Per-note expression](../expression-engine.md).
